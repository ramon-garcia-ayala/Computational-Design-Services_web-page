02  /  The bottleneck

AEC teams lose their best hours to work that software should be doing.

Not the hard parts. The parts that repeat.

  • Re-drawing the same site layout at every revision
  • Hand-counting quantities off a drawing set — then again after the set changes
  • Rebuilding an estimate workbook because a layer naming convention shifted
  • Chasing which model version a schedule was built from
  • Re-issuing a construction programme after one late equipment change

None of these need judgement. All of them need a person today — usually your most expensive one, because they're the only one who knows where the exceptions are.

Stop talking here and ask. "Which of these five happens in your office?" You want them naming the sixth one you didn't list — that is the project.
The last line is the hook: the senior person is doing the repetitive work precisely because they hold the exceptions. Automation doesn't replace them, it captures what only they know.

11  /  Where it doesn't — yet

Two cases where we'd tell you not to do this.

  • When the workflow runs twice a year. Frequency is most of the arithmetic on slide 9. Below a threshold, manual is simply correct — and we'd rather say that in the first call than in month three.
  • When you bill your own clients by the hour. Then automation cuts your invoice, not your cost, and the saving lands with your client instead of you. The case there is a different one — work you couldn't have bid on before, turned around inside a deadline you'd have declined — and we'd make that case rather than this one.

We'd rather lose a project on the first call than deliver one that measures out at nothing. Step 04 of our method commits us to reporting that number either way.

Counter-intuitive, and it is the highest-trust slide in the deck. Nobody in this industry disqualifies themselves out loud. Doing it makes every claim on the previous eleven slides more believable.
If they're an hourly-billing firm, this is not a dead end — pivot to capacity: "you take on the job with the impossible deadline." Different sale, same tooling.
Don't soften this. The moment you hedge it, it becomes a sales technique instead of a position.
