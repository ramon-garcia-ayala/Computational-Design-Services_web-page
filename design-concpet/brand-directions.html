Three brand directions,
measured before proposed.

The site works. What it does not yet have is a system — it has accumulated decisions. This document states what the audit actually found, then puts three complete directions against the same eight blocks so they can be compared rather than admired. Every contrast ratio printed here was computed, not estimated.

00

What the audit found

Five findings, each with the evidence that produced it. None of them is a matter of taste — they are places where the current system contradicts itself, and every direction below has to answer all five.

Finding 01 · Typography

Four typefaces are loaded. None of them is declared the brand face.

Home renders in Space Grotesk. Every other route renders in Sora with Inter. Both are defensible; having both, unlabelled, is not a system.

src/app/layout.tsx import { Sora, Inter, JetBrains_Mono, Space_Grotesk } from "next/font/google";
Finding 02 · Scale

Two type scales coexist, so the same heading has two rhythms.

Home and the design-lab panels scale fluidly. The rest of the site steps at breakpoints. Between two routes, an H1 changes behaviour, not just size.

home text-[clamp(1.28rem,4.96vw,2.56rem)] site text-4xl sm:text-6xl lg:text-7xl
Finding 03 · Colour

The brand colour is deleted on a third of the site.

Amber measures 1.00:1 against the pale ground — identical luminance, so literally invisible. The stylesheet works around it by remapping the accent to the foreground colour. On /about and /contact the brand colour is not dimmed; it is gone.

#e8a94a on #b8b4b1 → 1.00:1 (AA needs 4.50) [data-site-pale] .text-accent { color: var(--color-fg); }
Finding 04 · Conversion

Five conversion moments carry three different labels.

A visitor cannot learn one action. Worse, none of the three names what happens next — they name a social gesture, not an outcome.

hero "Let's talk" home closing "Contact us →" FinalCTA "Get in touch" footer "Get in touch" contact page "Get in touch"
Finding 05 · Message

Two brand promises compete, and neither is subordinated.

One is an outcome claim built for acquisition. The other is a positioning line built for craft. Both are good. A brand picks which one leads and makes the other support it.

site.ts tagline "We automate AEC. You ship faster." hero headline "Architecture, computed."
Finding 06 · Assets

The logo is one wordmark with no lockup rules.

A 3103×611 horizontal wordmark, applied as a CSS mask so it recolours per section — which is the right technique. But there is no stacked lockup, no icon lockup beyond the auto-cropped R² favicon, and no defined clear space or minimum size, so every new placement re-decides them.

public/logo/logo.png 164 KB, 3103 × 611 public/logo/logo-mask.png 7 KB, alpha only missing: stacked · icon · clear space · min size

One finding turned out to be structural, not cosmetic

The amber problem is usually read as "pick a darker amber". It is not fixable that way. The pale ground #b8b4b1 sits at relative luminance 0.4601 — mid-scale — so the maximum contrast anything can reach on it is 10.2:1, and that is pure black. A bronze that still reads as amber (#8a5a12) reaches only 2.87:1. Lightening the ground 40 % toward white still only gets it to 3.92:1.

So the honest conclusion is a rule, not a colour: on a mid-luminance ground, a saturated accent can never be body-size ink. It can be a filled plate with dark type on it (amber with #2a2826 measures 7.14:1), and it can carry large text or a UI border at 3:1. Each direction below states how it resolves this, because it is the single decision that determines whether the palette holds together.

The accent rule this direction adds

Amber is a dark-ground colour. On carbon and on the warm charcoal it is ink, a border and a focus ring. On the pale ground it appears only as a filled plate with #2a2826 type on it — never as ink, at any size.

That replaces today's silent remap to --color-fg, which does not dim the brand colour on /about and /contact — it removes it. A stated rule keeps the accent present as a shape even where it cannot be present as a colour.

Low

Direction B

Blueprint

The studio as an engineering consultancy. Inverts the site to a paper ground with ink-black type and a structural blue, and treats the dark carbon as the exception used for the hero and one closing panel.

For: the principal or BIM lead who has a budget and needs to justify the spend internally. Highest trust signal, best readability, and the only direction that survives being printed to PDF — which is how proposals actually circulate inside AEC firms.

Two notes, both honest

The accent is a real two-step ramp. #14568f on paper and #7fb4e6 on the inverse are the same colour at two lightnesses, both clearing AA on their own ground. That is the thing amber structurally cannot do on a mid-luminance greige.

The field border measures 2.69:1, under the 3:1 that WCAG 1.4.11 asks of a meaningful border. On a light ground a border dark enough to pass reads as a heavy box. The resolution is not a darker border: it is that fields carry a visible label and a 2px accent focus ring at 6.87:1, so the border is decorative and the state is carried by contrast that passes. Worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering later.

High

Medium

Recommendation

B for the business, A for the calendar — and they are not exclusive.

If the goal you stated is the one that decides this, Direction B is the answer. It is the only one where the accent problem disappears structurally rather than being managed with a rule, it is the only one that survives being printed and forwarded inside a firm, and its CTA is the only one of the three that names what the visitor gets. The cost is real: the default ground inverts and every section needs looking at.

Direction A is not a lesser version of B — it is a different bet. It says the site does not have an identity problem, it has a consistency problem, and fixes that for a fraction of the work. If the next few weeks belong to client work rather than the site, A is the honest choice and it makes B cheaper later, because collapsing the type system and fixing the border contrast are steps B needs anyway.

Direction C is the one to pick if differentiation is the actual constraint — if you are losing to studios that look more certain of themselves. It asks for the best copy of the three and it puts the geodesic sequence in question, so treat it as a decision about the whole site rather than a palette swap.

A path that avoids choosing badly: ship A now as consistency work, and use it to test whether the problem is coherence or positioning. If enquiries do not move, the problem was never the palette — and B's headline and CTA are the change worth making.
